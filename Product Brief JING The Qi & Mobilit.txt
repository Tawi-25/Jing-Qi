Product Brief
JING: The Personal Recovery Guardian
Version 2.0

Platform: Progressive Web App (PWA)
Primary Devices: Android, Desktop
Audience: Individuals living with chronic musculoskeletal pain, sedentary professionals, and anyone seeking a sustainable mobility practice.

1. Vision

Modern fitness apps reward intensity.

JING rewards longevity.

Rather than encouraging users to exercise harder or more frequently, JING acts as a personal recovery guardian, helping users move enough to improve mobility while protecting them from overexertion.

The philosophy is simple:

The healthiest body isn't the one that works the hardest. It's the one that recovers the best.

Movement is treated as medicine.

Rest is treated as productive.

Recovery is treated as measurable.

2. Mission

Help people maintain healthy joints, fascia, nerves, and mobility for decades by delivering intelligent daily movement recommendations that adapt to their body's current condition.

Instead of asking:

"How much can you do today?"

JING asks:

"What does your body need today?"

3. Design Philosophy

JING combines principles from modern movement science with insights from Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM).

Rather than presenting TCM as medical fact, the app uses it as a framework for creating mindful movement routines centered around recovery, balance, and body awareness.

Three guiding principles shape every feature inside the app.

Preserve Recovery Capacity

Recovery is a limited resource.

Every workout, stretch, or prolonged period of sitting places demands on the body.

JING helps users stay within a sustainable recovery capacity rather than constantly chasing improvement.

Restore Movement

Pain often causes people to move less.

Moving less usually causes more stiffness.

More stiffness often creates more pain.

JING aims to interrupt this cycle through small, consistent movement sessions instead of aggressive exercise.

Build Awareness

The goal isn't simply completing routines.

It's helping users recognize patterns.

Poor sleep.

High stress.

Long sitting sessions.

Increasing soreness.

Over time the user develops confidence in listening to their body rather than guessing what it needs.

Typical cycle:

Every feature should support at least one of these principles.

Protect before progressing.
Recovery is productive.

Consistency beats intensity.
Breath guides movement.

6. The Recovery Engine

The Recovery Engine is the heart of JING.

Instead of counting calories or rewarding exercise streaks, it continuously estimates how much recovery capacity the user has available.

Inputs include:

Morning soreness
Previous day's activity
Weekly movement load
Time spent sitting
Sleep quality (future)
Energy level (future)
Stress level (future)

The engine determines today's recommendation.

Green Day

Body appears well recovered.

Recommended:

Full mobility routine
Strength work
Walking
Breathwork
Yellow Day

Signs of accumulated fatigue.

Recommended:

Gentle mobility
Nerve glides
Walking
Breathwork

Avoid:

Heavy loading
Long routines
Red Day

Recovery is prioritized.

Recommended:

Guided breathing
Acupressure
Gentle positional relief
Short walks if comfortable

Avoid:

Stretching into pain
Intense exercise
Extended routines
7. The Recovery Capacity System

Instead of awarding points for doing more, JING tracks recovery demand.

Each activity contributes to the user's weekly movement load.

Example values:

Activity	Load
Breathing	0
Gentle mobility	1
Walking	1
Stability exercises	2
Longer strengthening routine	3

Load is adjusted dynamically based on current soreness.

For example:

A routine worth 2 points may count as 4 points if performed during a high-soreness day.

This encourages intelligent restraint rather than unnecessary effort.

9. Recovery Tools
Breath Pacer

A guided breathing interface available throughout the app.

Supported patterns:

4-7-8
4-2-6
Box Breathing

Breathing begins every movement session.

Acupressure Library

Visual guides explaining commonly used TCM points for comfort and relaxation.

Examples include:

GB30
BL40
Kidney 3

These are presented as optional complementary practices rather than medical treatments.

Flare-Up Mode

Designed for moments when pain is severe.

Instead of navigating menus, the user taps:

I'm Having a Flare-Up

The app immediately switches into recovery mode.

Sequence:

Calm breathing
Comfortable positions
Gentle nerve glides
Acupressure guidance
Walking recommendation if appropriate

No decision-making required.

12. Language Guidelines

JING should never shame or pressure users.

Avoid:

Push harder.
One more rep.
No excuses.
Keep grinding.

Prefer:

Your body may benefit from recovery today.
Rest supports progress.
Gentle movement is enough.
Listen before you load.

The app should feel like an experienced physiotherapist who understands recovery, not a fitness coach chasing personal bests.

13. Measuring Success

The app succeeds when users experience fewer setbacks, not more completed workouts.

Indicators include:

Reduced flare-ups
Improved consistency
Increased daily mobility
Less stiffness after sitting
Better awareness of recovery patterns
Greater confidence managing chronic pain

The ultimate measure of success is simple:

The user can live, work, and move with less pain and greater confidence.

15. Long-Term Vision

JING is not intended to become another fitness application.

It is intended to become a Personal Recovery Operating System.

Stretching is only one tool.

Over time, JING can evolve into an intelligent companion that understands how the user moves, recovers, works, and ages, helping them make better decisions every day.

Its purpose is not to maximize performance.

Its purpose is to preserve movement, reduce pain, and help people remain active throughout their lives.
